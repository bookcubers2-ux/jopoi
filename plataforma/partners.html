JOPÓI for institutions and partners

Jopói is a Guaraní word: reciprocal exchange. JOPÓI is open-source civic infrastructure, born in Santa Cruz, Bolivia, that turns public documents published as scanned images into braille a person can hold, audio, Easy Read and formal citizen proposals. It is run by the blind and neurodivergent young people it serves. This page is written in English for institutions, funders and legal partners. The platform itself is in Spanish.

The problem

Bolivia does not publish its laws; it publishes photographs of them. Every bill leaving the Departmental Legislative Assembly of Santa Cruz, which legislates for 3.5 million people, is a scanned PDF, and no screen reader can read an image. Across 85 formal requests filed by the blind community, the average wait for an accessible copy was 45 days. Law 977 has obliged every public institution since 2017 to employ at least 4% of people with disabilities and to provide them accessibility. Nine years on, no regulation says what that means. The law exists. It cannot be read, obeyed or enforced.

How it works

A scanned bill goes in. Text recognition runs inside the user's own device, so the document never leaves it and everything works offline after the first visit. Four things come out: Spanish grade 1 braille as a .BRF file for an embosser (or a slate-and-stylus guide where no embosser exists), spoken audio with native screen reader support, Easy Read to the UNE 153101 standard with pictograms, and a guided builder that turns a complaint into a formally structured proposal filed with the assembly. The app is static: no server, no database, no build step. The hundredth institution costs what the third one costs. Diagram and stack: Cómo funciona.

What has happened so far (as of 2 November 2026)

  • 420 unique users in the beta phase (29 May to 23 August 2026), across three municipalities; 1,150 sessions in the first 45 days, 40% from offline devices.
  • 355 bills, budget lines and draft laws scanned by blind users; 1,085 embossed braille pages hand-delivered to 45 people; 178 citizen proposals filed and received by standing committees.
  • 19 June 2026: first Mirror Session. Assembly members were blindfolded inside their own chamber and followed a real bill while 15 blind and neurodivergent fellows dictated every barrier from the legislators' seats. Broadcast live by the Assembly to more than 24,000 people.
  • 2 July 2026: Interinstitutional Technological Cooperation Agreement No. 045/2026 with the Assembly's Presidency, so accessible versions are produced at source.
  • 15 August 2026: Departmental Law No. 412 signed. The barriers dictated in the Mirror Session became law.
  • 23 August 2026: second Mirror Session, on neurodivergence, at the Law Faculty council of Universidad Autónoma Gabriel René Moreno. Barriers resolved on the spot rose from 1 in 10 (June) to 4 in 10 (August).
  • 15 young people certified as Civic Translators. One of them, who is blind, is now employed by the departmental government as Digital Accessibility Auditor. Before that hire, a government serving 3.5 million people employed two people with disabilities.

Every figure, with its denominator and source: Impacto. Open dataset of the 20 documented barriers (CC BY 4.0): datos-abiertos.

Licensing and brand

The code is released under the MIT licence and is replicable by anyone: a United Nations youth coordinator rebuilt it for Turkmenistan's parliament without asking us. The JOPÓI name and logo, the Mirror Session method and the Civic Translator certification identify the originating project and require written agreement to use. Copyright is held by Asociación Juvenil Bookcubers, a registered non-profit in Bolivia; external contributions are accepted under a contributor licence so that ownership stays single. A version already published under MIT remains free forever; the copyright holder decides the licence of future versions and may offer dual licensing if sustainability requires it. We do not plan to today, and the capacity exists. Full policy: Licencia y marca.

Data protection

Documents, braille, proposals, course progress and preferences are processed and stored only on the user's device. There are no accounts, no tracking cookies and no third-party analytics. The one exception is JOPOBOT, the optional voice assistant, which sends the user's question through a secure function to an AI provider; no name, email or scanned document is sent, and no conversation is kept on our servers. Full policy: Privacidad.

Business model

We do not sell software. We sell compliance. Law 977 binds every Bolivian public institution to an accessibility duty nobody has told them how to discharge. JOPÓI offers implementation, auditing, braille production and certified Civic Translators, at USD 1,200 per institution per year. The code is free for anyone to discharge the duty without us; what we sell is the human capacity nobody else has built. A public buyer has already paid for it: the departmental government has a JOPÓI-certified blind auditor on its payroll.

What we ask of legal partners

  1. A trademark and licensing structure for JOPÓI that works across several countries.
  2. A privacy and data protection policy that a public institution will accept, in Bolivia and in replicating countries.
  3. A contract template for the institutional compliance service.

Those three unblock our revenue and our spread across borders. Replication guide: Replicar JOPÓI. Source code: github.com/bookcubers2-ux/jopoi.

JOPÓI is implemented by Asociación Juvenil Bookcubers (Santa Cruz de la Sierra, Bolivia). Its first phase was funded by the Youth Democracy Network Mini Grants Program 2026 (USD 5,000), implemented by the Permanent Secretariat of the Community of Democracies. Contact: Carlos Olivera, Project Lead.
